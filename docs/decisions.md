# Prism — Decision Record

Canonical list of the founding decisions, taken with the owner on 2026-09-08 (#18 added 2026-09-14) in a one-question-at-a-time interview. #19–#25 were added on 2026-09-15 by the agents, under the owner's delegation, each on the evidence recorded in its ADR. #29 and #30 record the owner's review of the direction board on 2026-09-15: #29 the owner's decisions, #30 the semantic roles the board found. #31 records the owner's relicensing on 2026-09-22, which supersedes #28. #32 was taken on 2026-09-22 by the agents under the same delegation, on the evidence in ADR-0032, before Phase 4 wave 1 could multiply the problem it settles by four. #33 was taken on 2026-09-23 by the agents under the same delegation, with the direction board the owner signed off (#29) as its authority: Button.yaml had bound no outline width, and the two stacks had drawn different ones. #34 was taken on 2026-09-23 by the agents under the same delegation, before thirteen composites that host IconButton, and every later component with a badge slot, could copy a choice P4-4 had recorded only in its ticket. #35 was taken on 2026-09-23 by the agents under the same delegation, when the icon-metaphor audit's verification found that `filled` drew a different picture on each stack for 27 registry entries. #36 was taken on 2026-09-23 by the agents under the same delegation, before Avatar and Chip, the first two of nine specs that bind the glass-chip recipe, could decide for all nine how a part that is not a Surface draws glass and learns the ground it sits on. #37 was taken on 2026-09-24 by the agents under the same delegation, when the verification of the glass-chip shape (P4-5) found that a glass chip nested in a chip that renders its own cell draws a different picture on each stack, before Chip, the first component that nests one, is built. Each decision has a matching ADR in `docs/adr/` with fuller context and citations; if the two disagree, the ADR wins. Nothing below is "TBD": every line was explicitly chosen.

| 32 | **Component-owned strings are the app's, not Prism's. A component never invents a user-visible word: every string it renders or speaks is one the caller passed through a declared prop or an entry of a flat `strings` table the app owns — four templates in the whole system today (`Button.loading` `"{label}, loading"`, `Chip.remove` `"Remove {label}"`, `Badge.count` `"{count} {label}"`, `Badge.overflow` `"{max}+"`), keyed `<Component>.<name>` with the component segment in PascalCase so a key can never be read as a token path. Numbers, dates and measures come from the platform's locale formatter; where a stack's own primitive ships a localized string for the same element (React Aria's `Tag` remove label) Prism sets it explicitly, so both stacks say one word. | 36 | **The glass chip is a shape of the Surface module, and Backdrop declares the page over media. A component draws the one part whose background binds `material.glass.chip` through the Surface module's internal chip shape (`dsSurfaceChip` on Apple, `useSurfaceChip` on the web), handing it only that part's background cells by ground (`glass`, `own` or `none`), its shape, and at most an elevation, a gate (`content`, or `chrome` without the backdrop trigger) and a publication. The chip resolves with Surface's four triggers through one function per stack. It falls back to `color.bg.surface.raised` over `color.bg.page`, at the component's radius, with no top edge and never `inverse`. It publishes the ground it sits on, or `raised` under the fallback or on request, and every other part takes its cells from that, so no component reads a setting. A chip samples only the backdrop under its own shape, mirrored at its edges as Chromium does, and draws no backdrop filter on glass or inside another glass chip (inside any other chip; and under a chip that renders its own cell or its fallback, glass falls back: amended by ADR-0037). Apps declare media with `<Backdrop kind>` on the web or `.dsBackdrop(kind) { … }` on Apple, which publish the page over that kind and nothing else. The kind-less `dsBackdrop` is removed, and Apple's context setter becomes `package`. Every spec that binds the chip states `fallbackBackground: color.bg.surface.raised` over `fallbackUnderlay: color.bg.page`. `lint:literals`, the stylesheet test and `spec:validate` check all of it** (2026-09-23, amends 22, 25 and 29) | Nine specs bind the chip recipe, and three already say that the Surface module decides its fallback. One shape of that module keeps the rule in one place and reaches every part through the published context. A public Surface pill would publish `glass`, square its corners inside a card and turn `inverse` when selected. A glass module under Surface would re-plumb every glass baseline. Handing the resolution to each host would leave the fallback to each host's memory. Chromium blurs only a box's own backdrop, mirrored at its edges, so a chip that bled 3σ as the Apple Surface does would differ between the stacks at 32 pt. ## Process rules that follow from the decisions

1. A change to look or behavior begins in `spec/` (bump `specVersion`), then in `tokens/` if values move, then in each implementation. Never the other way round.
2. No raw hex, px or font names in implementation code. Only generated token accessors.
3. No icon without a registry entry. No color pair without passing the contrast test. No component without its accessibility block.
4. A platform marked `none` in a spec is not a TODO.

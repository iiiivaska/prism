Icon (`spec/components/Icon.yaml` 1): `<Icon name>` on the web and `DSIcon` in SwiftUI, one registry glyph in a square box that it never draws outside, at `sm`, `md` or `lg` in every density, with a `control` or `display` weight, an `outline`, `filled` or `duotone` style, and seven tones plus `inherit`, resolved against the material the enclosing Surface publishes. A glyph is not a control and takes no gesture. It has an accessible name only when the caller passes a `label`: the registry's `label` key is an id, never a name (ADR-0032). It is the one wave-1 component that also ships on watchOS. The web package also exports `glyphSizes`, `glyphWeights`, `glyphTones` and their types, and `DSGlyphTone` is public in DSCore.

Button's and Card's glyphs are now drawn by Icon, so the system has one glyph path. On Apple a glyph is the SF Symbol at the registry's calibrated size instead of a path stretched to its box: Button's glyphs take SF's proportions, the size Phosphor draws them on the web, and Card's open glyph goes from 14×13 to 11×10 pt around the same centre. On the web, the glyph in Card's icon ring now takes Icon's `primary` tone, as Apple already drew it. Four registry bindings changed: `object.map-pin` draws Phosphor's `map-pin-simple` on the web, `nav.open` is `arrow.up.forward` on Apple, and `object.calendar` and `object.chart` flip under `dir="rtl"` on the web, as SF Symbols already draws them in a right-to-left layout.

Chip (`spec/components/Chip.yaml` 1): `<Chip>` on the web and `DSChip` in SwiftUI, a small pill with one short `label` that filters, names a selection or identifies an entity, in `size` `sm` or `md` (the control height of that size). Its role follows from its props alone, the same on both stacks: a chip that sets `isSelected`, true or false, is a filter, a toggle button whose state that is (`aria-pressed` on the web, the selected trait on Apple), and its press asks the app, through `onPress`, to turn it on or off; one that leaves `isSelected` unset and has `onPress` or `isRemovable` is a button; one with none of the three is a static label. Selection thickens the stroke and lifts the label, never a solid fill. The leading position holds a `leadingIcon`, or on the md chip an `avatar`, Avatar's own `name`, `image` and `hasRing`, drawn small and decorative; `trailingIcon` shows what the press does. `isRemovable` draws a second button, the remove control, which fires `onRemove` and never `onPress`, as Delete and Backspace on the chip do, and is named by the app's `strings.Chip.remove` template, `"Remove {label}"` in English. Over a map, an image, a vivid surface or glass the pill is the Surface module's glass chip, its label and glyphs switch to the on-glass colours, and a selected chip adds a check, because every tone there is one colour; an Avatar inside it is drawn flat. Under Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast the pill falls back to the raised surface with the standard colours and no check. A press scales the pill to 0.97 and lays the pressed fill over whatever it renders, glass included; under Reduce Motion nothing scales. The web package also exports `chipSizes` and `chipKinds` and their types.
